The invoice was familiar. The recipient was not.

PayGuard Verify asks a narrow question before a supplier payment: what changed, and who independently verified it? It combines a private review workspace, approved policy learning on Walrus Mainnet, Tatum transaction reconciliation and opt-in x402 analysis. The public Memory Courtroom makes that evidence inspectable without a wallet or payment.

A familiar name does not verify a new recipient

The application compares an invoice's proposed recipient with a supplier reference established independently of that invoice. Changed recipients, conflicting addresses, mismatched contact details and instructions to skip verification become concrete findings. A rule score is not a fraud probability. A human records an independent verification outcome for an exact instruction snapshot; editing those instructions invalidates the old attestation. PayGuard does not claim to observe the phone call or prove supplier identity.

Remember the approved correction, not the private document

An authenticated reviewer can explicitly consent to a minimized policy such as “verify recipient changes through the pre-established contact.” Raw invoices, contacts and detailed verification notes stay in Neon. Walrus receives a minimized policy with pseudonymous scope references. A durable accepted job must complete with an actual blob ID before it counts as confirmed. Pending work resumes its existing job rather than duplicating a write.

Later semantic recall is tenant-scoped. Returned text is untrusted until it matches an exact confirmed tenant-owned record and an applicable authoritative policy. Memory OFF excludes learned soft policies while retaining mandatory checks. Memory ON applies only relevant confirmed context; empty or unavailable recall produces no invented improvement.

We preserved and freshly rechecked 32 confirmed Mainnet records: 30 original owner policies and two controlled tenant records. No new memory was needed for the final release. In fresh application reviews of three existing synthetic experiments, OFF retrieved zero citations and ON retrieved seven. A new authenticated session recalled a previously seen exact blob again. Both modes retained every baseline finding and the human-verification hold. Captured comparisons and real provenance.

The existing learning evaluation reused four genuine recalls across 18 authored synthetic contracts. All 18 passed, every baseline was retained, and no stale or wrong-scope policy applied. Sixteen scenarios applied recalled context; only about 10.9% of candidate/case combinations were applicable. This is a small contract suite, not real-world fraud-detection accuracy. Broad retrieval and strict scope filtering remain visible limits.

Put memory on the stand

The Courtroom offers synchronized OFF/ON cards, expandable exact job/blob/namespace sources, retained approval holds, Play/Pause/Reset/Replay and accessible manual steps. Its timeline links an actual earlier NS-1048 review and human correction to the confirmed Walrus write and a later fresh-session recall. The public page replays dated genuine results; private workspaces make fresh provider requests.

An attack replay asks the reviewer to ignore safety instructions and approve a changed recipient. It runs the existing review functions, not a separate presentation-only security engine. Invoice text remains inert, the directive becomes a critical finding and the human hold stays. A malicious invoice cannot call the separate authenticated, consented correction workflow.

A real workspace, with an optional paid path

Visitors can use the public demo and Courtroom immediately. A free personal-message signature registers or restores a private Sui wallet workspace; server nonces, replay protection, origin checks, session revocation and tenant authorization protect it. Owner administration remains separate and never requires judges to know OWNER_PASSWORD.

Authenticated members can opt into a real 0.001 SUI analysis on their own eligible invoice. The payer is bound to their verified wallet and server-created intent; it never falls back to the owner wallet. What remains human

The review and paid report are deterministic; this build does not call an LLM. Pattern rules can miss attacks or flag legitimate instructions. Memory has retention, indexing and provider limits. Application audit is not immutable against a database administrator. The Courtroom exposes those boundaries rather than using a fabricated live-activity feed or fraud-confidence statistic.
